For medical and dental admissions, MDCAT is the test that opens the door. It decides whether you qualify at all, and it carries half the weight of your aggregate. This guide explains the merit formula, the minimum scores, how the NUMS formula compares, and a practical way to prepare.

## What the MDCAT looks like

According to the criteria guide our merit calculator is built on, MDCAT follows a centralised PMDC policy for medical and dental colleges. The paper has:

- **200 MCQs** in total
- **Biology:** 68 questions
- **Chemistry:** 54 questions
- **Physics:** 54 questions
- **English:** 18 questions
- **Logical Reasoning:** 6 questions
- **No negative marking**

Biology alone is 68 of the 200 questions, and Chemistry and Physics together add another 108. Those three subjects decide most of your score.

## Minimum requirements

Before merit comes eligibility. Our source states:

- **FSc Pre-Medical:** a minimum of 60%.
- **Qualifying MDCAT score for MBBS:** 55%, which is 110 out of 200.
- **Qualifying MDCAT score for BDS:** 45%, which is 90 out of 200.

If your MDCAT score is below the qualifying mark for a programme, your aggregate does not matter for that programme. Make clearing the qualifying score your first milestone, then aim well beyond it.

## How MDCAT merit is calculated

The formula gives MDCAT 50%, FSc Pre-Medical 40% and Matric 10%. MDCAT is out of 200, FSc and Matric out of 1100.

A hypothetical example (made-up marks):

| Part | Fraction | × Weight | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| MDCAT | 150 ÷ 200 = 0.75 | × 50 | 37.50 |
| FSc Pre-Medical | 990 ÷ 1100 = 0.90 | × 40 | 36.00 |
| Matric | 1045 ÷ 1100 = 0.95 | × 10 | 9.50 |
| **Aggregate** | | | **83.00** |

Each MDCAT mark adds 50 ÷ 200 = 0.25 aggregate points. Every 10 FSc marks add 10 ÷ 1100 × 40 ≈ 0.36 points. Both matter, but by test time your FSc is mostly fixed, so the MDCAT is where you can still gain the most.

## How the NUMS formula compares

NUMS runs its own entry test, which our source says applies to Army Medical College and private military-affiliated colleges. Its formula uses the same weights with a different test total:

| | MDCAT | NUMS |
|---|---|---|
| Entry test total | 200 | 150 |
| Test weight | 50% | 50% |
| FSc Pre-Medical weight | 40% | 40% |
| Matric weight | 10% | 10% |
| Negative marking | None | None |
| Minimum FSc Pre-Medical | 60% | 60% |

Using the same hypothetical FSc and Matric marks, a NUMS score of 120 out of 150 gives 0.80 × 50 = 40.00 test points, for an aggregate of 85.50.

One more difference is worth knowing. The NUMS test has a Part A covering Biology, Chemistry, Physics and English, and a Part B psychological test. Our source calls Part B a mandatory evaluation component, but it is not part of the merit formula, so our calculator does not include it.

Our source guide does not state which admission year these formulas apply to, and formulas change between sessions. Always verify with the official PMDC, NUMS or college admission notification before you apply.

## A subject-wise way to prepare

### Biology

With the most questions on the paper, Biology deserves the most hours. Read your FSc textbook closely, because in an MCQ a single exact term or detail can separate the right option from a close distractor. Revise with short, frequent sessions rather than one long reread.

### Chemistry

Split your time between concepts (bonding, equilibrium, reaction types) and memory work (organic reactions, properties). Make one-page summaries per chapter and revisit them weekly.

### Physics

If Physics is your weakest subject, do not avoid it: 54 questions is too many to give away. Focus on understanding each formula, its units and when it applies, then build speed with timed numerical sets.

### English and Logical Reasoning

These 24 questions are fewer, but they are steady marks. Daily reading, grammar practice and a handful of reasoning puzzles keep them sharp without taking time from science.

### Putting it together

- Start with one timed baseline paper to find your weakest subject.
- Practise chapter-wise until each chapter feels familiar, then switch to mixed sets.
- Take full-length mocks under real conditions, and review every one the next day.
- Since there is no negative marking in the source format, answer every question.

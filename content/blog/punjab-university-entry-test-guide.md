The University of the Punjab weighs your academic record far more heavily than its entry test. That one fact changes how you should plan, both during FSc and in the weeks before the test. This guide explains the formula, what it means for you, and how to prepare.

## The PU merit formula

According to the criteria guide our merit calculator is built on, Punjab University uses:

- **PU entry test:** 25% of the aggregate, with the test marked out of 100
- **Academic score:** 75% of the aggregate, with Matric included in this academic share

In formula form:

```text
Aggregate = (PU Test score ÷ 100 × 25) + (Academic score ÷ Total academic × 75)
```

There is an important gap in our source. It says Matric is included in the academic score, but it does not say how Matric and FSc are combined, or what the total academic marks are. Check the official PU admission notification for your programme to find the exact method.

Our source guide does not state which admission year this formula applies to, and formulas change between sessions. Always verify with the official Punjab University admission website before you apply.

## What a 75 / 25 split means for you

Here is a hypothetical example. Suppose your academic record works out to 85% of its total (however PU combines it for your programme), and you score 70 out of 100 in the test:

| Part | Fraction | × Weight | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| PU Test | 70 ÷ 100 = 0.70 | × 25 | 17.50 |
| Academics | 85% = 0.85 | × 75 | 63.75 |
| **Aggregate** | | | **81.25** |

Now compare two improvements:

- **10 more test marks** (80 instead of 70) add 10 ÷ 100 × 25 = **2.50** points.
- **3 more percentage points in academics** (88% instead of 85%) add 0.03 × 75 = **2.25** points.

Each percentage point of academics is worth three test marks. So:

- **If you are still in FSc**, your board exams are your most powerful lever. A few extra percentage points in FSc can do as much as a big jump in the test.
- **If your results are already out**, the test is the only part you can still move. At 25% it still matters, especially when merit lists are tightly packed.

Punjab University is not the only academics-heavy formula. Quaid-i-Azam University gives academics 70% and its test 30% in our source, so the same thinking applies there.

## Know your test category and the rules

Our source describes PU entry tests as category-specific, for example PU-E for Pre-Engineering, PU-M for Pre-Medical and PU-C for Computer Science. Each test has 100 MCQs covering subject basics and general logic. There is no negative marking in the source format.

Eligibility minimums range from 50% to 60% depending on the department, and 60% is mandatory for PEC engineering programmes. Confirm the exact minimum for your programme before you apply.

## How to prepare for your PU test

### Start from your FSc textbooks

Because the test covers subject basics, your FSc textbooks are the best foundation. Revise chapter by chapter in the subjects your category tests, and focus on definitions, core formulas and standard concepts.

### Do not ignore general logic

Logic questions are quick to practise and easy to forget. A few reasoning puzzles a day keep this part sharp without taking time from your main subjects.

### Answer every question

With no negative marking in the source format, a blank answer can never score. Make your best attempt, mark questions you are unsure about, and come back to them if time allows.

### A simple four-week rhythm

- **Week 1:** take a timed baseline paper and list your weakest chapters.
- **Week 2:** practise those chapters one at a time and read every explanation.
- **Week 3:** switch to mixed sets and take one full mock under exam conditions.
- **Week 4:** take two or three more mocks, review each one, and ease off in the last two days.

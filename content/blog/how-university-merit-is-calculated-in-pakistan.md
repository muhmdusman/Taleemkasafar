Most university admissions in Pakistan come down to one number: your aggregate. It blends your Matric, FSc and entry test results into a single score, and the merit list is sorted by it. Once you know how that number is built, you can spend your preparation time on the part that moves it most.

## What an aggregate actually is

An aggregate is a weighted score out of 100. Each part of your record is turned into a fraction of its total, then multiplied by a weight the university chooses. The weights always add up to 100.

That makes an aggregate different from your average percentage. Two students with identical FSc marks can land far apart on a merit list, because the entry test often carries more weight than everything else combined.

The three usual ingredients are:

- **Matric / SSC** marks, usually the smallest share
- **FSc / HSSC / Intermediate** marks
- **Entry test** score, from a university test or a national one such as NET, ECAT or MDCAT

## The general formula

Almost every formula follows the same pattern:

```text
Aggregate = (Test marks ÷ Test total × Test weight)
          + (FSc marks ÷ FSc total × FSc weight)
          + (Matric marks ÷ Matric total × Matric weight)
```

Three things change from one university to the next:

1. The weights given to each part.
2. The entry test total. NET is marked out of 200, ECAT out of 400, and many university tests out of 100.
3. Whether Matric and FSc are counted separately or folded into one combined academic score.

## A worked example: NUST

The NUST formula in our calculator gives the NET 75%, FSc 15% and Matric 10%, with NET marked out of 200 and FSc and Matric out of 1100.

Take a hypothetical student (these marks are made up for illustration):

- NET: 140 out of 200
- FSc: 880 out of 1100
- Matric: 990 out of 1100

Now the arithmetic, one part at a time:

| Part | Fraction | × Weight | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| NET | 140 ÷ 200 = 0.70 | × 75 | 52.50 |
| FSc | 880 ÷ 1100 = 0.80 | × 15 | 12.00 |
| Matric | 990 ÷ 1100 = 0.90 | × 10 | 9.00 |
| **Aggregate** | | | **73.50** |

Here is the useful part. If the same student scores 20 more NET marks (160 out of 200), the NET share rises to 0.80 × 75 = 60.00, and the aggregate jumps by 7.50 to 81.00. By contrast, 55 extra FSc marks (935 instead of 880) would add only 0.75. At NUST, the entry test is where the aggregate is won or lost.

## How weights compare across universities

The weights below come from the criteria guide our calculator is built on. Some universities have more than one route; FAST-NUCES is shown with its NU Test route.

| University | Entry test (total) | Test | FSc / HSSC | Matric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NUST | NET (200) | 75% | 15% | 10% |
| UET Lahore | ECAT (400) | 33% | 50% | 17% |
| FAST-NUCES | NU Test (100) | 50% | 40% | 10% |
| GIKI | GIKI Test (total not stated) | 85% | 15% | Not counted |
| University of the Punjab | PU Test (100) | 25% | 75% academics | Included in academics |

A few patterns stand out:

- **Test-heavy:** NUST and GIKI put most of the weight on the entry test.
- **Academics-heavy:** UET Lahore gives FSc and Matric 67% between them, and Punjab University gives academics 75%.
- **Balanced:** FAST-NUCES (NU Test route) splits its weight evenly, 50% to the test and 50% to FSc and Matric together.

Our source guide does not state which admission year these formulas belong to, and formulas change between sessions. Always verify with the official university notification or admission website before you apply.

## Common confusions

### "Academics" can mean Matric and FSc together

Universities such as Punjab University and QAU use one combined academic score that already includes Matric. Our source does not explain how the two are merged, so check the university's own prospectus for the exact method.

### Entry test totals are not all the same

Divide by the total printed on your own result card, not by 100 out of habit. ECAT is out of 400, NET and MDCAT are out of 200, and the NUMS test is out of 150. A wrong total quietly ruins the whole calculation.

### Negative marking changes your score

ECAT gives +4 for a correct answer and −1 for a wrong one, so the number that goes into the formula is your net score after deductions. Our [ECAT strategy guide](/blog/ecat-preparation-strategy-uet-lahore) explains how to handle guessing.

### Board totals differ

The formulas in our source divide Matric and FSc by 1100. If your board uses a different total, use your actual obtained marks and your actual total so the fraction stays correct.

### Eligibility is a separate hurdle

Minimum marks are checked before merit. For example, UET Lahore asks for at least 60% unadjusted FSc marks, and NUST asks for at least 60% in both SSC and HSSC. A strong aggregate does not help if you miss a minimum.
